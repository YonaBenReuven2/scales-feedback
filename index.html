Sing along, see every note

Open one of your exercise MIDI files. The app works out the reference chords, the keys and what you sing from the file itself, so a new exercise from the generator works as soon as it exists.

Link the folder the generator writes to (python_final) and every exercise in it shows up here, new ones included. You can also drop .mid files anywhere on this page.

Settings

Sound

A zip of mNNN_name_vVVV.wav recordings, like the one recorded from the P-225. It stays in this browser and is never uploaded.

The files are written at full velocity; these set how hard the piano plays instead.

Listening

While your voice moves from one note to the next. Up to a third of a note at each end, so short notes are still judged.

If your pitch line below the keys trails the notes, raise the mic delay until they line up.

Hear me over the piano

With the P-225 connected by USB it shows up as an input called Line (P-Series).

Opened straight from the disk, Chrome hides the inputs and asks for each one, every time: once for the mic, then again for the piano (choose Line (P-Series) in its box). Open the app from its GitHub Pages address instead and it connects to both by itself.

For singing without headphones. The app learns how the piano's sound reaches your mic and subtracts it, so only your voice is left. Calibrate once with the mic where you sing: it learns every key in a few seconds and remembers them. A key it hasn't learned isn't scored while the piano plays it.

Open the filter lab

If the mic or the piano’s input won’t work, the report copies what the browser says about your inputs, to paste into a message. If it doesn’t hear you well over the piano, sing an exercise and then save the last 40 seconds: a sound file of what the mic and the piano’s audio heard, with what the app made of each moment, so the filter can be tuned to your room. The filter lab runs a short test and lets you hear what the filter leaves. Nothing is uploaded.
